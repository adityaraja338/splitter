On our way to Zero Point in Sikkim, we hit the classic group trip problem. 12 of us, everyone paying for random stuff, nobody sure who owed whom.

We usually use Kittysplit. Opened it, and the one feature we actually use, "who pays whom", is now paid. Great timing.

It's not a complicated app, so my first thought was to build one with Claude Code. Except I didn't have my laptop, and the network on those roads was barely working anyway.

So I tried it from my phone. Started a Claude Code session in the cloud and explained what I wanted, the way I'd explain it to a friend. Everything ran on a cloud machine, so my patchy network only had to carry the chat.

A few hours later we had our own splitter. Create a trip, share the link, add expenses, split them however you like, and it tells you who pays whom. Firebase, Firestore, no login, nothing fancy. The whole group used it for the rest of the trip.

The app isn't the point. What surprised me is how normal it felt to build it mid-trip, from the back seat of a car.

A tool going paid used to mean you paid or gave up. Now you can just make your own. That's the side of AI I find exciting: small everyday problems, solved by regular people.

If your group needs one, it's free to use: splitbox.web.app
And the code is open if you want to peek or host your own: github.com/adityaraja338/splitter
